Sinus Rhythm With 1st Degree AV Block: What It Means

Usually harmless

First degree AV block means every impulse from the upper chambers still reaches the lower chambers, but it takes slightly longer than normal to get through the AV node — a PR interval longer than 200 ms (one large square). Nothing is actually blocked. In most people it is a benign finding that needs no treatment.

What "first degree av block" means

The PR interval measures the time from the start of the P wave to the start of the QRS: the journey from the atria, through the AV node, to the ventricles. The AV node deliberately slows the signal, and in some people it slows it a little more than average. Fitness, ageing, medicines and high vagal tone all lengthen it.

Despite the name it is not a true block. Each P wave is followed by a QRS, just later than usual. The label "sinus rhythm with 1st degree AV block" simply pairs a normal rhythm with this long PR.

What it looks like on the ECG

A regular sinus rhythm in which the flat segment between the P wave and the QRS is consistently longer than one large square (200 ms), and the same on every beat.

Common causes

  • Athletic training and high vagal tone
  • Age-related slowing of the AV node
  • Beta-blockers, calcium-channel blockers, digoxin, amiodarone
  • Inferior heart attack
  • Myocarditis, Lyme disease, rheumatic fever
  • Electrolyte disturbance
  • Congenital

Should you worry?

Usually harmless

As an isolated finding without symptoms it carries little risk and needs no treatment. A very long PR (over 300 ms), a PR that is lengthening over time, dizzy spells or blackouts, or a wide QRS alongside it should be assessed, because those can point towards more advanced conduction disease.

Get urgent help if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

What usually happens next

  1. Usually nothing
  2. Review of heart-slowing medicines
  3. Repeat ECG at future routine checks
  4. Holter monitor if there are dizzy spells

Questions to ask your doctor

  • How long is my PR interval?
  • Is it stable compared with earlier ECGs?
  • Are any of my medicines contributing?

Frequently asked questions

Is first degree AV block dangerous?

Very rarely. Most people live normally with it and never progress to higher-grade block.

Can it go away?

Yes, if it was caused by a medicine or an acute illness. Vagal first degree block often disappears with exercise.

Is it the same as heart block?

It is the mildest form. Second and third degree block, where beats are actually dropped, are different and more important.
