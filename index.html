    How to use this site

    An ECG report has three layers: the measurements at the top, a list of automated findings, and an overall verdict such as Normal, Borderline or Abnormal ECG. The findings are generated by software that is deliberately over-sensitive, so a surprising number of alarming-sounding lines turn out to be harmless once a clinician reads the tracing. Start with how to read your ECG report, then look up each phrase individually.

    Every page follows the same structure — what the phrase means, what it looks like on the tracing, the common causes, an honest answer to "should I worry?", what usually happens next, and questions to bring to your appointment.
