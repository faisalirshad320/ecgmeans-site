Poor R Wave Progression: What It Means

Depends on context

"Poor R wave progression" means the upward R wave does not get taller as it should from chest lead V1 to V4 — typically the R wave in V3 is 3 mm or less. It can be a trace left by an old heart attack on the front of the heart, but far more often it reflects electrode position, body build or a normal variant.

What "Poor R wave progression" means

The six chest leads scan across the heart from right to left. In a normal tracing the R wave starts tiny in V1, grows steadily, and overtakes the downward S wave at around V3–V4 (the "transition"). When that growth is delayed, the software prints poor R wave progression, sometimes adding "consider anterior infarct".

It is a description, not a disease. Studies that followed up people with this finding show that only a minority have any scar on imaging.

What it looks like on the ECG

Small R waves persisting through V1–V3 with deep S waves, and the changeover to a dominant R wave happening late, in V5 or V6.

Common causes

  • Chest electrodes placed too high or too far to the left
  • Normal variant, more common in women
  • Obesity, breast tissue, emphysema or chest deformity
  • Left ventricular hypertrophy
  • Left bundle branch block or left anterior fascicular block
  • Old anterior or anteroseptal heart attack

Should you worry?

Depends on context

As an isolated finding in a person who feels well it carries little weight. It matters more alongside Q waves, T wave inversion in the same leads, cardiac risk factors or symptoms.

Get urgent help if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

What usually happens next

  1. Repeat ECG with careful lead placement
  2. Comparison with earlier tracings
  3. Echocardiogram if an old infarct is a realistic possibility

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Could the electrodes have been misplaced?
  • Are there any other signs of an old heart attack on this ECG?
  • Would an echocardiogram be useful?

Frequently asked questions

Is poor R wave progression normal?

It can be. A sizeable minority of healthy adults show it, particularly women and people with a broad or deep chest.

Does it mean a heart attack?

Only sometimes. It is one of the weaker ECG pointers to an old anterior infarct and needs imaging to confirm.

Can it be fixed?

There is nothing to fix unless an underlying cause is found. If it is due to lead position, a correctly recorded ECG will not show it.
