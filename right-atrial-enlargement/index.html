Possible Right Atrial Enlargement: ECG Meaning

Needs clinician review

"Possible right atrial enlargement" means the first part of your P wave — the bit made by the right upper chamber — is taller and more peaked than usual, a pattern called P pulmonale. Like its left-sided counterpart it is only a hint from the P wave shape; the ECG judges chamber size poorly, so an echocardiogram gives the real answer.

What "Right atrial enlargement" means

The P wave is made by the two atria contracting, the right one first. If the right atrium is enlarged or under pressure, its share of the P wave becomes taller and more pointed. The software flags a P wave taller than about 2.5 mm in the inferior leads (II, III, aVF), or a large initial positive deflection in V1. Many reports now use the broader term right atrial abnormality.

The right atrium enlarges mainly because of conditions downstream of it in the lungs and right ventricle, so right atrial enlargement often appears alongside signs of right ventricular hypertrophy.

What it looks like on the ECG

A tall, peaked P wave (over about 2.5 mm) in leads II, III and aVF — classic "P pulmonale" — or a tall initial positive component of the P wave in V1.

Common causes

  • Chronic lung disease and pulmonary hypertension
  • Tricuspid or pulmonary valve disease
  • Congenital heart disease
  • Right-sided heart failure
  • A normal variant or a slightly fast heart rate (false positive)

Should you worry?

On its own it is not an emergency. It is a marker that the right side of the heart may be under strain, usually from a lung condition, and that is what determines its importance. In a well person with no lung disease it is often an over-read of a normal, slightly peaked P wave.

Get urgent help if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

What usually happens next

  1. Review of any lung or heart symptoms
  2. An echocardiogram to measure the right atrium and estimate lung pressure, if warranted
  3. Assessment of the lungs where a chronic lung condition is suspected

Questions to ask your doctor

  • How confident is this finding on my tracing?
  • Is there any sign of a lung or right-heart problem?
  • Do I need an echocardiogram?

Frequently asked questions

What is P pulmonale?

The name for the tall, peaked P wave in the inferior leads that suggests right atrial enlargement, classically linked to lung disease.

How accurate is the ECG for right atrial enlargement?

Poor. It misses many enlarged atria and over-calls normal peaked P waves, which is why "possible" is printed and an echocardiogram is used to confirm.

Does it cause symptoms?

Not directly. Any symptoms come from the underlying lung or heart condition.
