@@LEDE@@
Ashman phenomenon is a single wide beat that happens when a short cycle follows a longer one, causing one beat to be conducted abnormally (aberrantly) through the bundle branches. It is most often seen in atrial fibrillation and is harmless in itself — its importance is that it can be mistaken for a premature ventricular beat.
@@BODY@@
## What "Ashman phenomenon" means

The time it takes the bundle branches to recover between beats depends on the previous beat's timing. After a longer gap, the recovery period lengthens. If a beat then arrives early (a short cycle after a long one), it may find one bundle branch — usually the right — still recovering, so it is conducted with a wide, abnormal shape. This normal quirk of conduction is Ashman phenomenon.

## What it looks like on the ECG

A single wide QRS, usually with a right bundle branch block shape, that follows a short R-R interval which itself followed a longer R-R interval. It is common in atrial fibrillation, where the irregular spacing provides the "long-short" sequence.

## Why it matters

Ashman beats are not dangerous, but they can look like premature ventricular contractions (PVCs) or short runs of ventricular tachycardia. Recognising the long-short cycle sequence and the typical right-bundle shape helps avoid unnecessary alarm and unnecessary treatment.

**Get checked** only if wide beats are frequent, sustained, or come with symptoms such as fainting, chest pain or severe breathlessness — to be sure they are aberrant beats and not a ventricular rhythm.

## Why it is mistaken for a PVC
An Ashman beat is wide and early, exactly like a premature ventricular contraction, so the two look alike at a glance. The clue is the preceding rhythm: an Ashman beat follows a short cycle that itself followed a longer one, and it usually has a right bundle branch block shape. Recognising this long-short sequence, which is common in atrial fibrillation, prevents a harmless beat from being mistaken for a ventricular rhythm.

## What usually happens next

1. Confirming the long-short cycle sequence that triggers the beat
2. Distinguishing it from true PVCs or ventricular tachycardia
3. No specific treatment for the Ashman beats themselves
4. Attention to the underlying rhythm, usually atrial fibrillation

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Are these wide beats aberrant conduction rather than PVCs?
- Do they need any treatment?
- Should the underlying atrial fibrillation be managed differently?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Is Ashman phenomenon dangerous?
A: No. It is a benign quirk of normal conduction; its main significance is that it can be mistaken for a premature ventricular beat or ventricular tachycardia.
Q: When does Ashman phenomenon occur?
A: When a short beat-to-beat cycle follows a longer one, most commonly during atrial fibrillation, causing one beat to conduct with a wide shape.
Q: Does Ashman phenomenon need treatment?
A: No specific treatment is needed for the aberrant beats; care focuses on the underlying rhythm such as atrial fibrillation.
