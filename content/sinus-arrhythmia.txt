@@LEDE@@
Sinus arrhythmia is a normal, harmless variation in which the heart speeds up slightly as you breathe in and slows as you breathe out. Despite the word "arrhythmia", it is not a rhythm disorder. It is most obvious in children, young adults and fit people, and indicates a healthy, responsive nervous system.
@@BODY@@
## What "sinus rhythm with sinus arrhythmia" means

Breathing changes the pressure in the chest and the activity of the vagus nerve, which in turn nudges the sinus node faster and slower. The software measures the spacing between beats, and if the longest and shortest intervals differ by more than about 120 ms (or 10%) it prints sinus arrhythmia. "Marked" just means the variation is larger.

The variation fades with age and in conditions such as diabetes that affect the autonomic nerves — so having it is, if anything, a good sign.

## What it looks like on the ECG

Every beat is normal, but the gaps between them lengthen and shorten in a repeating wave that follows the breathing cycle.

## Common causes

- Normal breathing (respiratory sinus arrhythmia)
- Youth and physical fitness
- Deep or slow breathing during the recording

## Should you worry?

Usually harmless

None. No restriction, treatment or follow-up is needed.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

@@FAQ@@
Q: Is sinus arrhythmia an irregular heartbeat?
A: Technically the spacing varies, but it is a normal physiological variation and not what doctors mean by an irregular heartbeat such as atrial fibrillation.
Q: Is it normal in children?
A: Yes. It is the rule rather than the exception in children and teenagers.
Q: Does it need treatment?
A: No.
