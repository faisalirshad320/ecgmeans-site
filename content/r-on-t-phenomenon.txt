## What "R-on-T phenomenon" means

Each heartbeat has a recovery phase, shown by the T wave, during which the heart muscle is partly reset and electrically unstable. The R wave is the tall spike of a beat. "R-on-T" describes a premature beat whose R wave lands on the preceding T wave — that is, it fires during this vulnerable period rather than waiting for full recovery.

## What it looks like on the ECG

A very early, wide PVC that interrupts or sits on the downslope or peak of the previous T wave, instead of appearing after the T wave has finished. The coupling interval (the gap from the normal beat to the PVC) is short.

## Why it can matter

During the vulnerable period, an extra beat can rarely set off ventricular tachycardia or ventricular fibrillation — fast, dangerous rhythms. This risk is higher when the heart is already irritable, for example soon after a heart attack, with a long QT interval, or with low potassium or magnesium. In a healthy heart, an isolated R-on-T beat is often harmless.

**Seek prompt care** if R-on-T beats come with palpitations that do not settle, fainting, chest pain or severe breathlessness.

## Why the vulnerable period matters
During the T wave, different parts of the heart muscle recover at slightly different times, so the tissue is briefly a patchwork of recovered and still-recovering cells. An impulse landing in this window can, rarely, start a self-sustaining circuit and trigger a fast ventricular rhythm. This is why the same early beat that is harmless in a healthy heart can be more dangerous soon after a heart attack or with a long QT interval.

## What usually happens next

1. A check for a long QT interval and for low potassium or magnesium
2. A review of the clinical context, such as recent heart attack or heart failure
3. Continuous monitoring if the heart is unstable or symptoms are present
4. Treating the cause — correcting electrolytes, adjusting medicines, or specific rhythm treatment

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Is my QT interval prolonged?
- Are my potassium and magnesium levels normal?
- Does my heart need monitoring right now?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Is R-on-T phenomenon dangerous?
A: It can occasionally trigger a dangerous fast rhythm, especially in an already-irritable heart, but a single R-on-T beat in a healthy heart is often harmless.
Q: What causes R-on-T beats?
A: Premature ventricular beats with a very short coupling interval, more likely with a long QT, low potassium or magnesium, or after a heart attack.
Q: How is it managed?
A: By correcting any electrolyte problems or long QT, treating the underlying heart condition, and monitoring if the heart is unstable.
