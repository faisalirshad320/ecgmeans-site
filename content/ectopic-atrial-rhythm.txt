@@LEDE@@
An ectopic atrial rhythm means the heartbeat is being started by a site in the atria other than the sinus node. The rhythm is regular and the QRS normal, but the P waves look different from usual — sometimes small, flat or inverted. At a normal rate it is often a harmless variant; it takes on more importance only when it is fast or persistent.
@@BODY@@
## What "Ectopic atrial rhythm" means

The sinus node is the usual pacemaker, but other atrial cells can take over if they fire faster, or if the sinus node slows. Because the impulse starts in a different place, it spreads through the atria on an unusual path, changing the shape of the P wave while leaving the QRS normal.

At 60–100 beats per minute this is simply an ectopic (or "low atrial") rhythm and is frequently seen in healthy people, especially the young, with high vagal tone. Above 100 it becomes ectopic atrial tachycardia, which is more likely to cause symptoms.

## What it looks like on the ECG

A regular rhythm with normal narrow QRS complexes but abnormally shaped P waves — for example P waves that are inverted in the inferior leads when the focus is low in the atrium.

## Common causes

- Normal variant, especially in young people with high vagal tone
- An escape rhythm when the sinus node slows
- Caffeine, alcohol, stress
- Heart or lung disease, when faster or sustained
- Digoxin toxicity (atrial tachycardia with block)

## Should you worry?

Depends on context

A slow or normal-rate ectopic atrial rhythm in a well person is usually benign and needs no treatment. A fast, sustained ectopic atrial tachycardia, or one causing palpitations, breathlessness or dizziness, deserves assessment, as does one in a person with known heart disease.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## What usually happens next

1. Comparison with previous ECGs
2. Reassurance if it is slow and the person is well
3. A Holter monitor if there are palpitations or the rhythm is fast
4. Assessment for an underlying cause if it is sustained

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Is this a benign variant for me?
- Was the rate normal or fast?
- Do I need a heart monitor?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Is an ectopic atrial rhythm dangerous?
A: Usually not at a normal rate in a healthy person. A fast, sustained ectopic atrial tachycardia is more likely to need treatment.
Q: What causes an ectopic atrial rhythm?
A: Often nothing more than a normal variant with high vagal tone; it can also follow sinus node slowing, stimulants, or heart and lung disease.
Q: Is it the same as a PAC?
A: Related but not identical. A PAC is a single early beat; an ectopic atrial rhythm is a run of beats driven by an atrial focus instead of the sinus node.
