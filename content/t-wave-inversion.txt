@@LEDE@@
T wave inversion means the T wave points downward instead of upward. In some leads (aVR, V1, and often III) that is completely normal. In others it can be a normal variant, a sign of strain from a thick ventricle, a bundle branch block effect, or evidence of reduced blood supply or a recent heart attack. Which leads are involved, and whether it is new, decide its meaning.
@@BODY@@
## What "t wave inversion" means

The T wave represents ventricular recovery. It normally points the same way as the QRS in most leads. When recovery is disturbed — by ischaemia, strain, inflammation or an abnormal activation sequence — the T wave flips. The pattern of leads involved maps onto the region of the heart affected.

Deep, symmetrical, arrowhead-shaped inversions in neighbouring leads are the ones that concern cardiologists, particularly in V2–V4 (Wellens pattern) after chest pain.

## What it looks like on the ECG

A T wave dipping below the baseline. Normal in aVR and V1; commonly normal in III and V2 in young people. Otherwise assess by lead group: anterior (V1–V4), lateral (I, aVL, V5–V6), inferior (II, III, aVF).

## Common causes

- Normal variant (aVR, V1, sometimes III, V2; persistent juvenile pattern)
- Left or right ventricular hypertrophy with strain
- Bundle branch block, paced rhythm, WPW
- Ischaemia or recent non-ST-elevation heart attack
- Pulmonary embolism (V1–V4 with right heart strain)
- Myocarditis, pericarditis
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
- Brain haemorrhage, digoxin, low potassium

## Should you worry?

Depends on context

Isolated inversion in a single lead, or in leads where it is expected, is usually benign. New inversion in a group of leads, especially with chest pain or breathlessness, needs same-day assessment. Deep inversion in the lateral leads of a young person should prompt an echocardiogram to look for cardiomyopathy.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## What usually happens next

1. Comparison with previous ECGs
2. Troponin blood test if there has been chest pain
3. Echocardiogram for unexplained or lateral inversion
4. Stress test or angiography if ischaemia is suspected

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Which leads show the inversion, and is it new?
- Does the pattern suggest strain, ischaemia or a variant?
- Do I need an echocardiogram?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Is T wave inversion always abnormal?
A: No. It is normal in aVR and V1 and frequently in lead III and V2.
Q: Can T wave inversion be caused by anxiety?
A: Anxiety and hyperventilation can flatten T waves or cause minor inversion, but deep inversion in multiple leads should not be attributed to anxiety without assessment.
Q: Does inverted T wave mean heart attack?
A: It can be a sign of ischaemia or a recent heart attack, but it has many other causes; the clinical context and troponin test decide.
