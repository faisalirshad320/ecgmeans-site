@@LEDE@@
Ventricular trigeminy means the ECG shows a repeating pattern of two normal beats followed by a premature ventricular contraction (PVC) — an early, wide beat from the ventricles. Like bigeminy, it is often harmless, especially in a normal heart, but a doctor should check how frequent the PVCs are and whether the heart is otherwise healthy.
@@BODY@@
## What "ventricular trigeminy" means

A premature ventricular contraction (PVC) is an extra beat that starts low in the heart. In ventricular trigeminy the pattern repeats in threes: normal – normal – PVC, normal – normal – PVC. "Tri-" means three, describing the group. It is the same type of extra beat as in bigeminy, just spaced out one PVC for every two normal beats.

## What it looks like on the ECG

A repeating group of two normal narrow QRS complexes (each with a P wave) followed by a wide, early QRS with no preceding P wave, usually with a short pause afterwards before the pattern starts again.

## Common causes

- Normal hearts — PVCs are extremely common
- Caffeine, alcohol, nicotine and stress
- Low potassium or magnesium
- Thyroid overactivity
- Underlying heart disease
- Some medicines and stimulants

## Should you worry?

Trigeminy in a structurally normal heart is usually benign. As with bigeminy, the key questions are how many PVCs occur over a day and whether the heart muscle and valves are normal. A very high PVC burden over time can weaken the heart and warrants follow-up.

**Get checked promptly** if trigeminy comes with fainting, chest pain, severe breathlessness, or a family history of sudden cardiac death.

## How common is it?
Like bigeminy, trigeminy reflects frequent PVCs and is a common, often harmless finding, particularly with caffeine, stress, tiredness or during illness, and it becomes more frequent with age. In a structurally normal heart it rarely causes problems, but if PVCs make up a large share of the day's beats they can, over time, affect the heart's pumping, so the overall burden is what matters most.

## What usually happens next

1. A review of symptoms, caffeine, alcohol and medicines
2. Blood tests for potassium, magnesium and thyroid function
3. A Holter monitor to measure the PVC burden
4. An echocardiogram if the burden is high or the heart may be abnormal

### Questions to ask your doctor

- How many PVCs am I having each day?
- Is my heart structurally normal?
- Do I need any treatment beyond cutting triggers?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Is ventricular trigeminy serious?
A: Usually not in a normal heart, but the PVC frequency and the underlying heart should be checked, because a very high burden can weaken the heart over time.
Q: What is the difference between bigeminy and trigeminy?
A: In bigeminy every second beat is a PVC; in trigeminy every third beat is a PVC (two normal beats then one early beat).
Q: Does trigeminy need treatment?
A: Often just reassurance and reducing triggers. Frequent or symptomatic PVCs may be treated with medication or ablation.
