@@LEDE@@
An EKG (ECG) records the heart's electrical activity over about ten seconds using stickers on the skin; it shows rhythm, conduction and electrical signs of strain or damage. An echocardiogram is an ultrasound scan that shows the heart's structure and pumping — chamber sizes, wall thickness, valves and ejection fraction. They answer different questions, which is why an abnormal EKG is often followed by an echo.

The EKG is the electrical tracing; the echocardiogram is an ultrasound picture of the heart itself.
@@BODY@@
## What "echocardiogram vs ekg" means

The EKG is quick, cheap and excellent for rhythm problems, conduction blocks and an acute heart attack. It is poor at judging chamber size or pump strength, and a normal EKG does not rule out structural heart disease.

The echocardiogram takes 20–45 minutes, uses no radiation, and directly measures how well the heart pumps and whether valves leak or narrow. It cannot capture an intermittent rhythm problem. Many of the "possible" statements on an EKG report — possible left atrial enlargement, possible LVH, septal infarct — are confirmed or dismissed by an echo.

## What it looks like on the ECG

EKG: a paper or screen tracing of waves. Echo: moving ultrasound images of the beating heart, with measurements and Doppler colour flow.

## Common causes

- EKG is used for: palpitations, chest pain, fainting, pre-operative checks, monitoring medicines
- Echo is used for: murmurs, breathlessness, heart failure, valve disease, abnormal EKG findings, family history of cardiomyopathy

## Should you worry?

Neither test carries risk. If you have had an abnormal EKG and your doctor has ordered an echo, that is the normal way of finding out whether the electrical finding reflects anything structural — it does not mean something serious has been found.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## What usually happens next

1. EKG first for rhythm or acute symptoms
2. Echo when structure or function is in question
3. Holter or event monitor if the problem is intermittent
4. Stress test if blood supply is the question

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Which test answers my particular question?
- If my EKG was abnormal, will an echo settle it?
- Do I need both?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Can an EKG detect a blocked artery?
A: It can show signs of an acute heart attack and sometimes of previous damage, but a normal EKG does not exclude blocked arteries. A stress test or angiogram is needed for that.
Q: Can an echocardiogram detect arrhythmia?
A: Only if the arrhythmia is happening during the scan. Rhythm assessment is the EKG's job.
Q: Which is better, EKG or echo?
A: Neither — they are complementary. An EKG looks at electricity; an echo looks at structure and pumping.
