## What "fragmented QRS" means

The QRS complex represents the ventricles being activated. When an area of muscle is scarred or diseased, the electrical wavefront has to detour around it, arriving in a broken-up fashion. This shows on the ECG as additional notches, spikes or an RSR'-like appearance within the QRS — a fragmented QRS — without the QRS necessarily being wide.

## What it looks like on the ECG

Extra deflections within the QRS: additional R or S waves, notching of the R or S wave, or a jagged, splintered shape, usually seen in two or more leads that look at the same region of the heart.

## Why it matters

Fragmentation often reflects a scar, for example from a previous heart attack, and it can also be seen in cardiomyopathies and other conditions. Research links fragmented QRS to a higher chance of arrhythmias and adverse events in certain heart conditions, so it is a useful, if non-specific, marker that adds to the overall picture rather than standing alone.

**Get checked** if fragmented QRS is found with chest symptoms, fainting, palpitations, or a known or suspected heart condition.

## A subtle but useful marker
Fragmentation is easy to overlook because the QRS does not have to be wide for it to be present — the clue is the extra notches or spikes within an otherwise ordinary-looking complex. On its own it is non-specific, but in someone with known or suspected heart disease it adds to the picture, and research has linked it to a somewhat higher risk of arrhythmias and scarring, which is why it is reported.

## What usually happens next

1. Interpretation in the context of your history and other ECG findings
2. An echocardiogram to look at heart muscle and scar
3. Further imaging, such as cardiac MRI, in selected cases
4. Management aimed at the underlying heart condition and arrhythmia risk

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Does my fragmented QRS suggest a scar or a cardiomyopathy?
- Does it change my risk or my treatment?
- Do I need further imaging such as an MRI?
@@FAQ@@
Q: What causes a fragmented QRS?
A: Scarred or diseased heart muscle — most often from a previous heart attack, but also from cardiomyopathies and other conditions — which splinters the electrical signal.
Q: Is a fragmented QRS dangerous?
A: The pattern itself is a marker, not a disease. It can indicate heart scar and a somewhat higher arrhythmia risk, so it is assessed with your history and other tests.
Q: Does a fragmented QRS need treatment?
A: There is no treatment for the pattern itself; care is directed at the underlying heart condition it points to.
