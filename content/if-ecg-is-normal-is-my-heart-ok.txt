@@LEDE@@
A normal ECG is reassuring: it makes an acute heart attack, a serious rhythm disturbance during the recording, and significant conduction disease unlikely. But it is a ten-second electrical snapshot. It cannot see blocked arteries that are not currently causing damage, mild valve disease, or a rhythm problem that comes and goes. Whether your heart is OK depends on what question is being asked.
@@BODY@@
## What "if ecg is normal, is my heart ok" means

The ECG is very good at what it does — recording electrical activity at one moment. Most coronary artery disease produces no ECG change at rest; up to half of people with significant narrowings have a normal resting ECG. Palpitations that are not happening during the recording leave no trace.

That is why doctors match the test to the question: a stress test or CT angiogram for blood supply, an echocardiogram for structure and valves, a Holter or event monitor for intermittent rhythm problems.

## What it looks like on the ECG

Sinus rhythm, normal intervals and axis, no ST or T abnormalities, no abnormal Q waves, and a verdict of Normal ECG.

## Common causes

- Things a normal ECG does not exclude: stable coronary artery disease, intermittent arrhythmia, mild to moderate valve disease, early cardiomyopathy, high blood pressure damage, heart failure with preserved ejection fraction

## Should you worry?

If you have no symptoms, a normal ECG plus a normal examination is good news. If you have exertional chest tightness, breathlessness, fainting or palpitations, a normal ECG should not end the assessment; the right further test depends on the symptom.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## What usually happens next

1. Symptoms on exertion: stress test or CT coronary angiogram
2. Palpitations: Holter, patch or event monitor
3. Murmur or breathlessness: echocardiogram
4. Risk factors: blood pressure, cholesterol and diabetes management regardless of the ECG

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Does a normal ECG answer the question I came with?
- Do my symptoms need a different test?
- What is my overall cardiovascular risk?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Can you have a heart attack with a normal ECG?
A: Yes. A small proportion of heart attacks show a normal initial ECG, which is why blood troponin tests and repeat ECGs are used.
Q: Can a normal ECG rule out blocked arteries?
A: No. A resting ECG is often normal in stable coronary disease.
Q: How often should I have an ECG?
A: There is no fixed schedule for healthy people. It is done when symptoms, risk factors or a procedure call for it.
