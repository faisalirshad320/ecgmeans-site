@@BODY@@
Bigeminy is a rhythm *pattern*, not a disease in itself. The word comes from the Latin for "twin", because the beats arrive in pairs. Most of the time the extra beat is a PVC, which is why "bigeminy PVC" or "PVC bigeminy" is the usual way it is described. This page explains what a bigeminy ECG looks like, how ventricular and atrial bigeminy differ, what causes it, and when it needs investigation.

## What "Bigeminy" means

Each premature beat resets the timing in a way that favours another premature beat after the next normal one, so the pattern tends to sustain itself for seconds or minutes. The early beat often pumps little blood, which is why a pulse check or a fitness watch may report half the true heart rate.

Atrial bigeminy is the same pattern with PACs and carries the same benign outlook.

### Bigeminy PVCs: why the extra beat is usually ventricular

A PVC ([premature ventricular contraction](https://www.ecgmeans.com/premature-ventricular-contractions/)) is an early beat from the ventricles, the heart's main pumping chambers. When a PVC follows every normal beat, the result is ventricular bigeminy. Because PVCs are so common, PVC bigeminy is the form most often printed on ECG reports and Holter summaries. For a page focused only on that form, see [ventricular bigeminy](https://www.ecgmeans.com/ventricular-bigeminy/).

## Bigeminy ECG: what it looks like

Alternating narrow normal beats and wide, tall, early beats with no P wave, each followed by a pause.

When reading a bigeminy ECG strip, look for:

1. **A normal beat** — P wave, normal PR interval, narrow QRS.
2. **An early beat straight after it**, arriving before the next P wave is due.
3. **A pause** after the early beat — usually a full compensatory pause with PVCs.
4. **The same pair repeating** — normal, early, pause — for at least several cycles.

If every early beat has the same shape, the PVCs are coming from one spot (unifocal). If the shapes vary, the report may say [multifocal PVCs](https://www.ecgmeans.com/multifocal-pvcs/), which is looked at more carefully.

## Ventricular vs atrial bigeminy

Both produce the same "pairs" rhythm, but the extra beat starts in a different place.

| Feature | Ventricular bigeminy (PVC bigeminy) | [Atrial bigeminy](https://www.ecgmeans.com/atrial-bigeminy/) (PAC bigeminy) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the extra beat starts | Ventricles (lower chambers) | Atria (upper chambers) |
| P wave before the extra beat | None | Present, but early and often a different shape |
| QRS of the extra beat | Wide (120 ms or more) and bizarre | Usually narrow, like a normal beat |
| T wave of the extra beat | Usually points opposite to the QRS | Usually normal |
| Pause afterwards | Usually a full compensatory pause | Usually a shorter, non-compensatory pause |
| Usual outlook in a normal heart | Generally benign | Generally benign |

If you are unsure which one you have, the [PVC vs PAC](https://www.ecgmeans.com/pvc-vs-pac/) page shows the differences side by side.

## Bigeminy vs trigeminy vs couplets

| Pattern | What repeats | Example sequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bigeminy | One normal beat, one PVC | N-V-N-V-N-V |
| [Trigeminy](https://www.ecgmeans.com/trigeminy/) | Two normal beats, one PVC | N-N-V-N-N-V |
| Quadrigeminy | Three normal beats, one PVC | N-N-N-V-N-N-N-V |
| [Couplet](https://www.ecgmeans.com/ventricular-couplet/) | Two PVCs in a row | N-V-V-N |
| Non-sustained VT | Three or more PVCs in a row | N-V-V-V-N |

(N = normal beat, V = ventricular premature beat.) Runs of three or more PVCs are a separate finding — see [ventricular tachycardia](https://www.ecgmeans.com/ventricular-tachycardia/).

## Is bigeminy the same as an irregular heartbeat or AFib?

Bigeminy does make the pulse feel irregular, but it is a *regularly irregular* rhythm: the same short-long pattern repeats. [Atrial fibrillation](https://www.ecgmeans.com/atrial-fibrillation/) is *irregularly irregular* — there is no repeating pattern at all, and there are no clear P waves on the ECG. The two are easy to tell apart on a proper tracing, which is why an ECG is more useful than a pulse check or a watch reading. Bigeminy does not cause the stroke risk linked with atrial fibrillation.

## What bigeminy feels like

Many people feel nothing. Others notice:

- A thumping, flip-flopping or "skipping" feeling in the chest
- A pulse that seems slow or irregular
- A strong beat after each pause
- Light-headedness, tiredness or breathlessness if it lasts a long time

Because the early beat pumps little blood, a home blood pressure monitor, pulse oximeter or smartwatch may read a heart rate of 35–45 even though the heart is beating 70–90 times a minute. A full ECG shows the true picture.

## Bigeminy on a Holter monitor vs a standard ECG

A standard ECG records only about ten seconds, so it can catch bigeminy by chance or miss it entirely. A Holter monitor or patch records every beat for one to several days and reports:

- **Total PVC count** and **PVC burden** (the percentage of all beats)
- **How many episodes of bigeminy** occurred and how long the longest one lasted
- **Whether the PVCs have one shape or several**
- **What time of day** they happen and whether they line up with symptoms you note in a diary

This is the information that decides whether bigeminy needs any treatment. A single ECG showing bigeminy says little about how often it happens over a normal day.

## Common causes

- Caffeine, alcohol, nicotine, stimulants
- Stress, poor sleep, exercise recovery
- Low potassium or magnesium
- Overactive thyroid
- Digoxin toxicity
- Coronary disease, cardiomyopathy or a previous heart attack

Other contributors can include dehydration, decongestants, some asthma inhalers, high blood pressure with a thickened heart muscle, and heart valve disease. Often no single trigger is found.

## Should you worry?

Short runs in a healthy person are common and harmless. It warrants assessment if it is persistent, if you have fainting, chest pain or breathlessness, or if you have known heart disease. A very high PVC burden — typically above 10–15% of all beats over 24 hours — can gradually weaken the heart muscle and is treatable.

Sustained bigeminy can mean a very high burden. If every second beat were a PVC for a whole day, the PVCs would make up about half of all beats. In practice, bigeminy usually comes and goes, which is why a 24-hour or longer recording is used to measure the real percentage.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## Bigeminy in particular situations

| Situation | What doctors consider |
| --- | --- |
| Healthy adult, normal echocardiogram | Usually reassurance and trigger reduction |
| Taking digoxin | Bigeminy can be an early sign of digoxin toxicity, so a blood level and potassium are usually checked — see [digoxin effect on an ECG](https://www.ecgmeans.com/digoxin-effect-ecg/) |
| Previous heart attack or weak heart muscle | Closer follow-up, because frequent PVCs can be a marker of the underlying disease |
| During or soon after a heart attack | Monitored in hospital alongside treatment of the heart attack |
| Endurance athlete | Usually checked with an echocardiogram and an exercise test before clearance |
| Low potassium or magnesium | Correcting the level often reduces the extra beats |

### Practical steps that often help

1. Cut back on caffeine, energy drinks and alcohol for a few weeks to see whether the pattern changes.
2. Stop smoking or vaping nicotine.
3. Aim for regular sleep and manage stress where you can.
4. Stay well hydrated, especially in hot weather or after exercise.
5. Check any cold remedies or supplements with a pharmacist, as some contain stimulants.
6. Keep a simple diary of when you feel the extra beats — it helps your doctor match symptoms to the recording.

## What usually happens next

1. Electrolytes and thyroid tests
2. 24-hour Holter to count the percentage of extra beats
3. Echocardiogram to confirm normal structure and pumping
4. Treatment (beta-blocker or ablation) only for symptoms or a high burden

In practice, your doctor will also ask about caffeine, alcohol, sleep and medicines, and may check whether the bigeminy fades when your heart rate rises with exercise — a reassuring sign in many people. If you take digoxin, a blood level is usually checked, because bigeminy can be an early sign of digoxin toxicity.

### Questions to ask your doctor

- What percentage of my beats are PVCs?
- Is my heart structurally normal?
- Do I need treatment or just reassurance?
- Is it ventricular or atrial bigeminy?
- Are all my extra beats the same shape?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Is bigeminy dangerous?
A: With a normal heart on echocardiogram, generally not.
Q: What is the difference between bigeminy and trigeminy?
A: In bigeminy every second beat is premature; in trigeminy every third.
Q: Why does my watch show a heart rate of 35–40?
A: Wrist sensors often fail to detect the weak premature beat, so they count only the normal ones.
Q: Can bigeminy go away on its own?
A: Yes. Bigeminy often comes and goes over minutes or hours, and it may settle once triggers such as caffeine, stress or low potassium are dealt with.
Q: Can anxiety cause bigeminy?
A: Stress and anxiety raise adrenaline levels, which can make PVCs and bigeminy more frequent. They are a common trigger in otherwise healthy people.
Q: How is PVC bigeminy treated?
A: Most people need no treatment beyond reducing triggers. When symptoms are troublesome or the PVC burden is high, your doctor may consider medicines such as a beta-blocker, or catheter ablation of the spot causing the extra beats.
