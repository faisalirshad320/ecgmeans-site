@@LEDE@@
Right bundle branch block (RBBB) means the impulse is delayed in the right branch of the heart's wiring, so the right ventricle is activated a little late. The QRS widens and takes on an "M-shaped" rSR' pattern in lead V1. Incomplete RBBB (QRS 110–119 ms) is a common normal variant, particularly in young people; complete RBBB is usually harmless too but is worth checking once.
@@BODY@@
## What "right bundle branch block" means

The right bundle is a long, thin structure and is easily delayed. When it is, the left ventricle fires normally and the right one follows slightly later, producing a second upstroke in V1 (the R') and a wide S wave in the lateral leads. Because the left ventricle — the main pumping chamber — is activated normally, RBBB rarely affects heart function.

Incomplete RBBB is found in a few percent of healthy young adults and athletes. It reflects slight delay rather than true block.

## What it looks like on the ECG

A QRS of ≥120 ms (complete) or 110–119 ms (incomplete), an rSR' pattern in V1–V2, and broad, slurred S waves in leads I, V5 and V6.

## Common causes

- Normal variant (especially incomplete RBBB)
- Ageing of the conduction system
- Right heart strain — pulmonary embolism, lung disease, pulmonary hypertension
- Atrial septal defect and other congenital conditions
- Coronary disease or previous heart attack
- Myocarditis, cardiomyopathy

## Should you worry?

Usually harmless

Incomplete RBBB without symptoms needs nothing. Complete RBBB found by chance in a well person is also usually benign, though a one-off echocardiogram is reasonable. A new RBBB with breathlessness or chest pain, or RBBB combined with a fascicular block or a long PR interval, deserves prompt assessment.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## What usually happens next

1. Often nothing for incomplete RBBB
2. One-off echocardiogram for new complete RBBB
3. Further tests only if symptoms or other findings

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Is this complete or incomplete?
- Is it new?
- Is an echocardiogram needed?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Is incomplete right bundle branch block serious?
A: No. It is a normal variant in the great majority and does not restrict activity.
Q: Can RBBB cause symptoms?
A: Not by itself. Symptoms come from an underlying cause, if there is one.
Q: Does RBBB turn into heart block?
A: Isolated RBBB rarely progresses. The risk is higher when it is combined with left-sided fascicular block (bifascicular block).
