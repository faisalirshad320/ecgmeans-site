Normal adult values: heart rate 60–100 bpm, PR interval 120–200 ms, QRS under 120 ms, QTc under about 450 ms in men and 460–470 ms in women, and axis −30° to +90°. ## EKG interpretation cheat sheet: the reading order

Use the same order on every tracing. Our step-by-step guide to [how to read an EKG strip](https://www.ecgmeans.com/how-to-read-an-ekg-strip/) explains each step.

1. Calibration (25 mm/s, 10 mm = 1 mV)
2. Rate
3. Rhythm (regular or irregular)
4. P waves (present? one per QRS? upright in II?)
5. PR interval
6. QRS width
7. Axis (12-lead)
8. QT / QTc
9. ST segments, T waves, Q waves, R-wave progression

## Paper and grid

| Unit | Time | Voltage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small box (1 mm) | 0.04 s (40 ms) | 0.1 mV |
| Large box (5 mm) | 0.20 s (200 ms) | 0.5 mV |
| 5 large boxes | 1 s | — |
| 30 large boxes | 6 s | — |

## Normal adult EKG values

| Measurement | Normal | Abnormal flags |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heart rate | 60–100 bpm | < 60 bradycardia; > 100 tachycardia |
| P wave duration | < 120 ms (3 small boxes) | Wide or notched: left atrial enlargement |
| P wave height (II) | < 2.5 mm | ≥ 2.5 mm: right atrial enlargement |
| PR interval | 120–200 ms (3–5 small boxes) | > 200 ms: 1st-degree block; < 120 ms: pre-excitation or junctional |
| QRS duration | < 120 ms (usually 80–100 ms) | ≥ 120 ms: bundle branch block, ventricular or paced beat |
| QTc | < ~450 ms men; < ~460–470 ms women | > 500 ms: high risk of torsades |
| QRS axis | −30° to +90° | < −30° left axis deviation; > +90° right axis deviation |
| ST segment | Level with baseline | Elevation or depression: see ST rules below |
| T wave | Upright in I, II, V3–V6; inverted in aVR | Peaked, flat or inverted: see context |
| R-wave transition | V3–V4 | Poor R-wave progression |

Upper QTc limits vary slightly between references. Full detail is in [normal ECG values](https://www.ecgmeans.com/normal-ecg-values/), and the [QTc calculator](https://www.ecgmeans.com/qtc-calculator/) does the correction for you.

## Heart rate quick table

300 ÷ large boxes between R waves, or 1500 ÷ small boxes. For an irregular rhythm: QRS count in 6 s × 10. You can also use the [ECG heart rate calculator](https://www.ecgmeans.com/ecg-heart-rate-calculator/).

| Large boxes R to R | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rate (bpm) | 300 | 150 | 100 | 75 | 60 | 50 | ~43 | ~38 |

## Rhythm recognition table

| Rhythm | Rate (bpm) | Regularity | P waves | PR | QRS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal sinus rhythm | 60–100 | Regular | Upright in II, 1 per QRS | 120–200 ms | Narrow |
| Sinus bradycardia | < 60 | Regular | Normal | Normal | Narrow |
| Sinus tachycardia | > 100 | Regular | Normal (may merge with T) | Normal | Narrow |
| Sinus arrhythmia | 60–100 | Varies with breathing | Normal | Normal | Narrow |
| Atrial fibrillation | Variable (often fast) | Irregularly irregular | None; wavy f waves | None | Narrow |
| Atrial flutter | Atria ~300; ventricles often ~150 | Regular or variable | Sawtooth F waves | Not measurable | Narrow |
| SVT (e.g. AVNRT) | 150–250 | Regular | Hidden or retrograde | Not measurable | Narrow |
| Multifocal atrial tachycardia | > 100 | Irregular | ≥ 3 shapes | Varies | Narrow |
| Junctional rhythm | 40–60 | Regular | Absent, inverted or after QRS | < 120 ms if seen | Narrow |
| Ventricular tachycardia | > 100 (often 150–250) | Regular | Dissociated or absent | None | Wide (≥ 120 ms) |
| Ventricular fibrillation | Not measurable | Chaotic | None | None | No true QRS |
| Idioventricular rhythm | 20–40 | Regular | None | None | Wide |
| Asystole | 0 | Flat line | None (or P only) | None | None |

Worked examples of each are in our [ECG rhythm strips](https://www.ecgmeans.com/ecg-rhythm-strips/) library.

## Heart block recognition

| Block | Key feature | Dropped beats? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st-degree AV block | PR > 200 ms, constant | No |
| 2nd-degree Mobitz I (Wenckebach) | PR lengthens beat by beat until a QRS drops | Yes, in a repeating pattern |
| 2nd-degree Mobitz II | PR constant; sudden dropped QRS | Yes, unpredictably |
| 2:1 AV block | Every 2nd P wave has no QRS | Yes; cannot tell type I from II on one strip |
| 3rd-degree (complete) block | P waves and QRS march independently; P-P and R-R each regular | P waves never conduct |
| Right bundle branch block | QRS ≥ 120 ms; rSR′ in V1; wide S in I and V6 | — |
| Left bundle branch block | QRS ≥ 120 ms; broad notched R in I, aVL, V5–V6; deep S in V1 | — |
| Left anterior fascicular block | Axis −45° or beyond; qR in aVL; rS in II, III, aVF | — |
| Left posterior fascicular block | Right axis; rS in I; qR in III (after excluding other causes) | — |

More: [third-degree AV block](https://www.ecgmeans.com/third-degree-av-block/) and [LBBB vs RBBB](https://www.ecgmeans.com/lbbb-vs-rbbb/).

## Axis quick rules (leads I and aVF)

| Lead I | Lead aVF | Axis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positive | Positive | Normal (0° to +90°) |
| Positive | Negative | Left axis. If lead II is positive, still normal (0° to −30°); if II is negative, left axis deviation |
| Negative | Positive | Right axis deviation |
| Negative | Negative | Extreme axis ("northwest"). Think ventricular rhythm or lead misplacement |

Try it with the [ECG axis calculator](https://www.ecgmeans.com/ecg-axis-calculator/).

## STEMI territories

ST elevation of at least 1 mm in two contiguous leads. In V2–V3 the thresholds are ≥ 2 mm in men aged 40 and over, ≥ 2.5 mm in men under 40, and ≥ 1.5 mm in women.

| Territory | Leads | Usual artery | Reciprocal changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Septal | V1–V2 | LAD | — |
| Anterior | V3–V4 | LAD | Inferior leads |
| Lateral | I, aVL, V5–V6 | Circumflex or diagonal | III, aVF |
| Inferior | II, III, aVF | RCA (sometimes circumflex) | I, aVL |
| Posterior | ST depression + tall R in V1–V3; ST elevation in V7–V9 | RCA or circumflex | — |
| Right ventricle | V4R (with inferior STEMI) | Proximal RCA | — |

ST elevation in aVR with widespread ST depression suggests diffuse ischemia or left main disease. See [ST elevation](https://www.ecgmeans.com/st-elevation/) and [STEMI vs NSTEMI](https://www.ecgmeans.com/stemi-vs-nstemi/).

## Electrolytes and drugs

| Condition | EKG changes (in rough order of severity) |
| --- | --- |
| Hyperkalemia (high potassium) | Peaked T → flat or lost P, long PR → wide QRS → sine wave → VF or asystole |
| Hypokalemia (low potassium) | Flat T, ST depression, prominent U wave, long QU interval |
| Hypercalcemia (high calcium) | Short QT (short ST segment) |
| Hypocalcemia (low calcium) | Long QT (long ST segment) |
| Digoxin effect | "Scooped" (sagging) ST depression, short QT |

Details: [hyperkalemia ECG](https://www.ecgmeans.com/hyperkalemia-ecg/) and [hypokalemia ECG](https://www.ecgmeans.com/hypokalemia-ecg/).

## Mnemonics worth knowing

| Mnemonic | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **300-150-100-75-60-50** | Rate from large boxes between R waves |
| **WiLLiaM MaRRoW** | LBBB: W in V1, M in V6. RBBB: M in V1, W in V6 |
| **"Longer, longer, longer, drop"** | Mobitz I (Wenckebach) |
| **"Two thumbs up"** | Leads I and aVF both positive = normal axis |
| **"Leaving / Returning"** | QRS in I and aVF pointing apart = left axis; pointing toward each other = right axis |
| **"White on right, smoke over fire"** | US limb lead colours: white RA; black (smoke) LA above red (fire) LL |
| **"Any wide-complex tachycardia is VT"** | Until proven otherwise |

## Red flags on any strip

- Wide-complex tachycardia, VF, asystole or pulseless rhythms: start the emergency response
- New ST elevation meeting the criteria above
- Complete heart block or Mobitz II
- QTc over 500 ms
- Peaked T waves with a wide QRS (suspect severe hyperkalemia)

**Get urgent help** if a person has chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness or a racing heartbeat. Call 911 (US), 999 (UK) or 112 (EU). Do not rely on a cheat sheet in an emergency.

@@FAQ@@
Q: What should an EKG cheat sheet include?
A: The normal intervals (PR, QRS, QTc), a rate table, rhythm and heart block features, the lead I and aVF axis rule, STEMI lead territories and the main electrolyte patterns.
Q: What are the normal EKG intervals?
A: PR 120–200 ms, QRS under 120 ms, and QTc under about 450 ms in men or 460–470 ms in women. Above 500 ms is clearly prolonged.
Q: What is the fastest way to calculate rate on an EKG?
A: For a regular rhythm, divide 300 by the number of large boxes between R waves. For an irregular rhythm, count QRS complexes in 6 seconds and multiply by 10.
Q: Which leads show an inferior MI?
A: Leads II, III and aVF, usually from a blocked right coronary artery, often with reciprocal ST depression in I and aVL.
Q: How do you tell Mobitz I from Mobitz II?
A: In Mobitz I the PR interval gets longer before a dropped beat. In Mobitz II the PR stays constant and a beat drops without warning. Mobitz II is more likely to progress to complete block.
Q: Can I use an EKG cheat sheet to read my own EKG?
A: It can help you understand the terms, but it is not for self-diagnosis. Your clinician reads the tracing together with your symptoms, history and previous EKGs.
