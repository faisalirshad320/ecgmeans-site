@@LEDE@@
An ECG report has three parts: a block of measurements (rate, PR, QRS, QT/QTc, axis), a list of automated findings, and an overall verdict such as Normal, Borderline or Abnormal ECG. The measurements are reliable; the findings are over-sensitive suggestions; the verdict is only as good as the most significant finding. This guide walks through each part.
@@BODY@@
## The short answer

Measurements: heart rate 60–100; PR 120–200 ms; QRS under 120 ms; QTc under about 440–460 ms; P axis, QRS axis (−30° to +90°) and T axis. Anything outside these limits generates a finding line.

Findings: each line names a rhythm, a conduction feature or a wave abnormality. Common ones are covered on this site. The words "possible", "consider", "cannot rule out" and "age undetermined" all signal low confidence.

Verdict: driven by the findings. "Unconfirmed report" or "physician over-read required" means a clinician has not yet reviewed it. The clinician's own interpretation, usually written or signed on the tracing, overrides the computer.

## What it looks like on the ECG

A standard 12-lead printout shows twelve short strips arranged in four columns of three (I, II, III / aVR, aVL, aVF / V1–V3 / V4–V6) and one long rhythm strip along the bottom, usually lead II. One small square is 0.04 s across and 0.1 mV tall.

## Common causes

- Rate: count large squares between R waves and divide 300 by that number
- Rhythm: is there a P before every QRS, and a QRS after every P?
- Axis: are I and aVF both upright? If so the axis is normal
- Intervals: PR, QRS, QTc against the limits above
- Waves: any Q waves, tall or small R waves, ST shifts, T inversions?

## Should you worry?

Reading the report yourself is a good way to prepare questions, not a substitute for the clinician's read. If a line worries you, look it up here, note whether it is a low-confidence phrase, and ask specifically about it.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## What usually happens next

1. Find each phrase on this site to see what it typically means
2. Check whether the finding appears on an earlier ECG
3. Ask your clinician the three questions listed on each page

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Has a clinician over-read this ECG?
- Which of these lines matters, if any?
- Can I have a copy for my records?
@@FAQ@@
Q: What does "unconfirmed" mean on an ECG?
A: That the automated interpretation has not yet been checked by a clinician.
Q: Are ECG machine readings accurate?
A: The measurements are. The interpretation lines are deliberately sensitive and over-call abnormalities, so they need human confirmation.
Q: What is a normal ECG reading?
A: Sinus rhythm at 60–100 with a PR of 120–200 ms, QRS under 120 ms, QTc under 440–460 ms and an axis between −30° and +90°, with no significant wave abnormalities.
