## What "2:1 AV block" means

In second degree AV block some atrial impulses reach the ventricles and some are blocked. When the pattern is exactly two-to-one, every second P wave has no QRS after it. Because you cannot see the tell-tale gradual PR lengthening of Mobitz I (there is never a second conducted beat in a row to compare), a 2:1 strip is ambiguous.

Clues help: a narrow QRS and a long PR on the conducted beats suggest the AV-node-level, more benign Mobitz I; a wide QRS and a normal PR suggest the His-Purkinje-level Mobitz II, which is more likely to progress to complete heart block. A longer recording, or watching the response to exercise, usually settles it.

## What it looks like on the ECG

Regular P waves at a steady rate, but only every second P wave is followed by a QRS, so the ventricular rate is exactly half the atrial rate. The conducted beats have a constant PR interval.

## Common causes

- Age-related degeneration of the conduction system
- Heart attack
- Heart-slowing medicines (beta-blockers, calcium-channel blockers, digoxin)
- After cardiac surgery
- High vagal tone (more likely the benign type)

## Should you worry?

Seek prompt care

2:1 AV block is taken seriously because it may be the Mobitz II type, which can progress without warning to complete heart block. It is usually assessed promptly, especially if there is a wide QRS, fainting, dizziness or a very slow pulse, and it may lead to a pacemaker.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with fainting, severe dizziness, chest pain, breathlessness, or a very slow pulse. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## What usually happens next

1. A longer ECG recording to classify the block
2. Review of heart-slowing medicines
3. An exercise test — Mobitz I often improves with exertion, Mobitz II worsens
4. A pacemaker if it is the Mobitz II type or causes symptoms

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Is this the Mobitz I or Mobitz II type?
- Is my QRS narrow or wide?
- Could a medicine be responsible, and do I need a pacemaker?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Is 2:1 AV block dangerous?
A: It can be, because it may be the Mobitz II type that can progress to complete heart block. It is always assessed carefully.
Q: Why can't a single ECG tell Mobitz I from Mobitz II in 2:1 block?
A: Because the distinguishing feature — gradual PR lengthening before a dropped beat — needs two conducted beats in a row, which 2:1 conduction never provides.
Q: Does 2:1 AV block need a pacemaker?
A: Often, if it is the Mobitz II type or causes symptoms. A reversible cause such as a medicine is looked for first.
