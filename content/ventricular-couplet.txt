@@BODY@@
## What "ventricular couplet" means

A premature ventricular contraction (PVC) is an early beat from the ventricles. When two PVCs occur consecutively with no normal beat between them, it is called a couplet (or pair). Three or more in a row is instead called a run of ventricular tachycardia, so a couplet is the two-beat version.

## What it looks like on the ECG

Two wide, early QRS complexes in a row, each without a preceding P wave, usually different in shape from the normal beats, followed by a pause before the rhythm resumes.

## Common causes

- Normal hearts, especially with caffeine, alcohol, nicotine or stress
- Low potassium or magnesium
- Overactive thyroid
- Underlying heart disease, such as a previous heart attack or cardiomyopathy
- Some medicines and stimulants

## Should you worry?

An occasional couplet in a structurally normal heart is usually not dangerous. The significance rises when couplets are frequent, when they occur with known heart disease, or when they come with symptoms, because they indicate a more irritable ventricle. Your doctor will look at how often they happen and whether the heart is otherwise healthy.

**Get prompt care** if couplets come with fainting, chest pain, severe breathlessness, or a family history of sudden cardiac death.

## Where it sits on the spectrum
It helps to picture PVCs on a scale: a single PVC, then a couplet (two in a row), then a run of three or more, which is called ventricular tachycardia. A couplet is therefore one step along from an isolated extra beat. In a healthy heart this step is usually of little consequence, but with underlying heart disease the same finding is watched more closely, because it shows a readier tendency to string beats together.

## What usually happens next

1. A review of symptoms and triggers such as caffeine and medicines
2. Blood tests for potassium, magnesium and thyroid function
3. A Holter monitor to count couplets and look for longer runs
4. An echocardiogram if the heart may be abnormal or the burden is high

### Questions to ask your doctor

- How frequent are my couplets, and are there longer runs?
- Is my heart structurally normal?
- Do I need treatment or just monitoring?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Is a ventricular couplet dangerous?
A: An occasional couplet in a normal heart is usually harmless, but frequent couplets or couplets with heart disease deserve assessment because they reflect a more irritable ventricle.
Q: What is the difference between a couplet and ventricular tachycardia?
A: A couplet is exactly two PVCs in a row; three or more consecutive ventricular beats is called ventricular tachycardia.
Q: How are frequent couplets managed?
A: By correcting triggers and electrolytes, treating any underlying heart disease, and sometimes medication or ablation if they are frequent or symptomatic.
