## What "anteroseptal infarct age undetermined" means

The septum and front wall of the left ventricle are supplied by the left anterior descending artery and viewed by leads V1 to V4. Healthy muscle there makes an R wave that grows steadily across the chest leads. If that growth fails or Q waves appear, the program suspects old scar and, when both the septal and anterior leads are involved, prints anteroseptal infarct.

"Age undetermined" means there is no sign of a heart attack in progress — only a fixed pattern that could be old. The same appearance is produced very often by electrodes placed too high on the chest, by a thick or rotated heart, and by left bundle branch block, so the finding is frequently a false positive.

## What it looks like on the ECG

Q waves or QS complexes, or small R waves that fail to grow, across leads V1–V4. A genuine old infarct usually shows the change in several neighbouring leads, sometimes with T wave abnormalities.

## Common causes

- Chest electrodes placed too high (a very common explanation)
- Left ventricular hypertrophy or a rotated heart
- Left bundle branch block or left anterior fascicular block
- Poor R wave progression as a normal variant
- A genuine previous anteroseptal heart attack

## Should you worry?

A real anteroseptal infarct reflects significant previous damage to a large part of the pumping chamber, so the line should be looked at by a clinician. But because lead placement and body build mimic it so readily, you should not assume you have had a heart attack from the ECG alone. An echocardiogram and comparison with old tracings decide it.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## What usually happens next

1. Clinician over-read and comparison with earlier ECGs
2. A repeat ECG with careful, correct chest-lead placement
3. An echocardiogram to look for a thin or poorly moving front wall
4. Risk-factor treatment and cardiology follow-up if scar is confirmed

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Could the chest electrodes have been placed too high?
- Is this new compared with my previous ECG?
- Should I have an echocardiogram to settle it?
@@FAQ@@
Q: What is the difference between anterior, septal and anteroseptal infarct?
A: Only the leads involved. Septal is V1–V2, anterior is V3–V4, and anteroseptal involves both.
Q: Can anteroseptal infarct on ECG be a false alarm?
A: Yes, commonly. Poor R wave progression from lead placement or body build produces the same picture in healthy people.
Q: How is it confirmed?
A: With an echocardiogram and by comparing the ECG with earlier tracings.
