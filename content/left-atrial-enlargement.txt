@@LEDE@@
"Possible left atrial enlargement" means the P wave — the small bump before each QRS — is wider or more notched than usual, a shape that can occur when the left upper chamber of the heart is stretched or under pressure. The ECG is a poor judge of chamber size, so this is a hint, not a measurement; an echocardiogram gives the real answer.
@@BODY@@
## What "left atrial enlargement" means

The P wave is the electrical signature of the two atria contracting, right first and then left. If the left atrium is larger or conducts slowly, its part of the P wave is delayed and prolonged. The software flags a P wave longer than 120 ms, a double-humped P wave in lead II, or a deep, wide negative tail in lead V1. Many reports now use the more accurate term left atrial abnormality.

The left atrium enlarges when it has to push against resistance or handle extra volume over years — most commonly from high blood pressure, and also from mitral valve disease or a stiff left ventricle.

## What it looks like on the ECG

A broad, notched "M-shaped" P wave in lead II, or a P wave in V1 whose second half dips down for more than one small square.

## Common causes

- Long-standing high blood pressure
- Mitral valve narrowing or leakage
- Stiff or weakened left ventricle (heart failure)
- Obesity and sleep apnoea
- Slow conduction within a normal-sized atrium (a false positive)

## Should you worry?

It is not an emergency. Its importance is as a marker: a genuinely enlarged left atrium raises the long-term chance of atrial fibrillation and tells your doctor that blood pressure or a valve may need attention.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## What usually happens next

1. Blood pressure measurement and review of any treatment
2. Listening for a heart murmur
3. Echocardiogram to measure the atrium directly, if clinically warranted

### Questions to ask your doctor

- How confident is this finding on my tracing?
- Should I have an echocardiogram?
- Is my blood pressure where it should be?
@@FAQ@@
Q: How accurate is the ECG for left atrial enlargement?
A: Not very. It misses many enlarged atria and flags many normal ones, which is why the word "possible" is printed.
Q: Can left atrial enlargement be reversed?
A: Treating the cause — especially lowering blood pressure, treating sleep apnoea and losing weight — can reduce atrial size in some people.
Q: Does it cause symptoms?
A: Not directly. Symptoms come from the underlying cause or from atrial fibrillation if that develops.
