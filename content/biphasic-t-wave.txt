@@LEDE@@
A biphasic T wave is one that goes in two directions — partly up and partly down — instead of being a simple single hump. What it means depends on the order and the leads: an up-then-down T wave in the chest leads after chest pain can be an important warning sign (Wellens pattern), while a down-then-up T wave is more often linked with low potassium or a minor change.
@@BODY@@
## What "Biphasic T wave" means

The T wave reflects the ventricles recovering after each beat. When that recovery is disturbed unevenly, the T wave can take on two phases. The direction matters. An initial upward deflection followed by a terminal downward dip in leads V2–V3, in someone who has had chest pain, is the Wellens pattern and points to a critical narrowing of the main front artery. A downward-then-upward T wave is more typical of low potassium or digoxin.

Because one pattern can be a red flag and another is minor, biphasic T waves are always read in the context of symptoms and the leads involved.

## What it looks like on the ECG

A T wave with two components. Up-then-down in V2–V3 (type A Wellens) is the concerning pattern; down-then-up is more often benign or electrolyte-related.

## Common causes

- Ischaemia, including the Wellens pattern after chest pain (up-then-down in V2–V3)
- Low potassium or magnesium
- Digoxin effect
- Left ventricular hypertrophy with strain
- A normal variant in some leads

## Should you worry?

Depends on context

A biphasic T wave in the front chest leads after an episode of chest pain should be treated as a possible warning of serious coronary disease and assessed promptly, even if you now feel well. A biphasic T wave with low potassium is corrected by treating the potassium. Context decides.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## What usually happens next

1. Review of symptoms and comparison with old ECGs
2. A troponin test if there has been chest pain
3. Potassium and magnesium blood tests
4. Cardiology assessment and possibly angiography if a Wellens pattern is suspected

### Questions to ask your doctor

- Which leads show the biphasic T wave, and after what symptoms?
- Could this be the Wellens pattern?
- Should my potassium be checked?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Is a biphasic T wave serious?
A: It can be. An up-then-down T wave in the front chest leads after chest pain (Wellens) is a warning sign; other patterns are more often minor or electrolyte-related.
Q: What is Wellens syndrome?
A: An ECG pattern of biphasic or deeply inverted T waves in V2–V3 after chest pain, signalling a critical narrowing of the left anterior descending artery.
Q: Can low potassium cause biphasic T waves?
A: Yes, along with prominent U waves and ST changes.
