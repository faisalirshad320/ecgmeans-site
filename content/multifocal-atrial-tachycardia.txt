@@LEDE@@
Multifocal atrial tachycardia (MAT) is a fast, irregular rhythm in which several different sites in the atria fire in competition, producing at least three distinct P wave shapes at a rate over 100. It is strongly associated with serious lung disease and other acute illness, so although the rhythm itself is rarely the main danger, it is a marker that the person is unwell.
@@BODY@@
## What "multifocal atrial tachycardia" means

In MAT at least three separate atrial pacemaker sites fire independently, each producing its own P wave shape, at a combined rate above 100 beats per minute. The irregular timing can make it look like atrial fibrillation, but unlike AF there are clear (if varying) P waves before the QRS complexes.

MAT most often appears in people with severe chronic lung disease, especially during a flare-up with low oxygen, and in other acute illnesses. Treating the underlying illness is usually what settles the rhythm.

## What it looks like on the ECG

An irregular rhythm over 100 beats per minute with at least three different P wave shapes and varying PR intervals. Each QRS is preceded by a P wave, which distinguishes it from atrial fibrillation.

## Common causes

- Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), especially during an exacerbation
- Low blood oxygen and acute illness
- Heart failure and coronary disease
- Low potassium or magnesium
- Theophylline and some other medicines

## Should you worry?

MAT is usually a sign that someone is significantly unwell, most often from a lung problem, so the focus is on finding and treating that cause. The rhythm itself seldom needs specific anti-arrhythmic treatment and often resolves once the illness improves. It is managed in a medical setting rather than ignored.

**Get urgent help** if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

## What usually happens next

1. Assessment and treatment of the underlying illness, especially the lungs
2. Checking and correcting oxygen, potassium and magnesium
3. Review of medicines such as theophylline
4. Rate-controlling medication (often a calcium-channel blocker) if needed

### Questions to ask your doctor

- What underlying illness is driving this rhythm?
- Are my oxygen and electrolyte levels normal?
- Will it settle once the illness is treated?
@@FAQ@@
Q: Is multifocal atrial tachycardia serious?
A: The rhythm is seldom the main danger, but it usually signals serious underlying illness such as a COPD flare, so it is taken seriously.
Q: How is it different from atrial fibrillation?
A: Both are irregular, but MAT has clear P waves (at least three shapes) before the QRS complexes, while atrial fibrillation has none.
Q: How is it treated?
A: Mainly by treating the underlying illness and correcting oxygen and electrolytes; rate-controlling medication is used if necessary.
