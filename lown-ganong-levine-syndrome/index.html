Lown-Ganong-Levine (LGL) Syndrome

Needs clinician review

Lown-Ganong-Levine (LGL) syndrome is an old term for a short PR interval on the ECG together with a normal, narrow QRS (no delta wave) and episodes of fast heartbeat. It is a form of suspected pre-excitation. The concept is debated today, but if your report mentions it, the key issue is the fast-heartbeat episodes, which deserve assessment.

What "Lown-Ganong-Levine syndrome" means

LGL syndrome was described as the combination of a short PR interval (under 0.12 seconds), a normal narrow QRS without a delta wave, and bouts of fast heartbeat (paroxysmal tachycardia). It was thought to involve a pathway that bypasses the slowing function of the AV node while still using the normal conduction system below it, which is why the QRS stays normal — unlike Wolff-Parkinson-White, which has a delta wave.

What it looks like on the ECG

A consistently short PR interval with a normal-width QRS and no delta wave. Between episodes the ECG is otherwise normal; the fast rhythms themselves are usually a regular supraventricular tachycardia.

Why it matters and the modern view

Modern electrophysiology has shown that a short PR with a normal QRS can arise from several mechanisms, and "LGL syndrome" is no longer considered a single, well-defined disease. What matters clinically is not the label but whether you have symptomatic fast-heartbeat episodes, which can usually be diagnosed precisely and treated.

Seek prompt care for episodes of a very fast, pounding heartbeat, especially with chest pain, breathlessness, dizziness or fainting.

Why the term is used cautiously

When LGL was first described, a short PR with a normal QRS was assumed to mean a specific bypass pathway. Later electrophysiology studies showed that a short PR can arise from several mechanisms, including simply fast conduction through a normal AV node. For this reason many cardiologists now avoid the label and focus instead on precisely diagnosing and treating any episodes of fast heartbeat.

What usually happens next

  1. Confirming the short PR and ruling out a delta wave (which would suggest WPW)
  2. Capturing a tachycardia episode on a monitor if possible
  3. An electrophysiology study if episodes are frequent or troublesome
  4. Treatment of the specific tachycardia, which may include medication or ablation

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Do I truly have pre-excitation, or just a short PR interval?
  • What is the exact cause of my fast-heartbeat episodes?
  • Would an EP study or ablation help me?

Frequently asked questions

What is Lown-Ganong-Levine syndrome?

A described combination of a short PR interval, a normal narrow QRS without a delta wave, and episodes of fast heartbeat. It is now considered a loosely defined concept rather than a single disease.

How is LGL different from Wolff-Parkinson-White?

WPW has a delta wave and a widened QRS from pre-excitation of the ventricle; LGL has a short PR but a normal, narrow QRS with no delta wave.

Is a short PR interval always LGL syndrome?

No. A short PR can have several causes and is only called LGL when it occurs with episodes of fast heartbeat; the symptoms, not the label, guide care.
