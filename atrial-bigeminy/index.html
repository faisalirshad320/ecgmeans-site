Atrial Bigeminy: What It Means on an ECG

Usually harmless

Atrial bigeminy means the ECG shows a repeating pattern where every normal beat is followed by a premature atrial contraction (PAC) — an early beat from the upper chambers. It is very common, usually harmless, and often felt as skipped beats. A doctor may still check for triggers if it happens a lot.

What "atrial bigeminy" means

A premature atrial contraction (PAC) is an early extra beat that starts in the atria, above the AV node. In atrial bigeminy, PACs alternate one-for-one with normal beats: normal – PAC – normal – PAC. It is the atrial equivalent of ventricular bigeminy, but the extra beats come from the top of the heart and are usually narrow.

What it looks like on the ECG

A regular alternation between a normal beat and an early beat whose P wave looks different from the normal one (it may be a different shape or hidden in the previous T wave). The early beat's QRS is usually narrow and normal-looking, and is often followed by a short pause.

Common causes

  • Normal hearts — PACs are extremely common and increase with age
  • Caffeine, alcohol, nicotine and stress
  • Tiredness and poor sleep
  • Overactive thyroid
  • Electrolyte imbalances
  • Sometimes underlying heart or lung disease

Should you worry?

Atrial bigeminy is usually benign and often needs nothing more than reassurance. Very frequent PACs can occasionally be a marker for a tendency to atrial fibrillation, so a doctor may want to know how often they occur, especially if you have palpitations or other symptoms.

Get checked if atrial bigeminy comes with fainting, chest pain, severe breathlessness, or episodes of a fast, irregular heartbeat.

What it can feel like

Many people are unaware of atrial bigeminy, while others feel a skipped beat, a brief flutter, or a thump as the heart resets after each early beat. These sensations can be more noticeable at rest or when lying down at night. They are usually harmless, though a sudden increase in extra beats, or a change in how they feel, is worth mentioning to your doctor.

What usually happens next

  1. Reassurance and a review of caffeine, alcohol and stress
  2. Blood tests for thyroid function and electrolytes if symptoms warrant
  3. A Holter monitor if palpitations are frequent
  4. Follow-up for atrial fibrillation if PACs are very frequent

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Are my extra beats harmless?
  • Could they lead to atrial fibrillation?
  • Should I cut back on caffeine or other triggers?

Frequently asked questions

Is atrial bigeminy dangerous?

It is usually harmless, especially in a normal heart, though very frequent PACs can occasionally be a marker for a tendency toward atrial fibrillation.

What does atrial bigeminy feel like?

Many people feel skipped or extra beats or a brief flutter; many feel nothing at all.

Does atrial bigeminy need treatment?

Usually just reassurance and reducing triggers like caffeine; treatment is considered only if symptoms are troublesome.
