EKG Interpretation Cheat Sheet (ECG Quick Reference)

This page puts the most useful EKG facts in one place: the numbers you have to know, how to recognise the common rhythms and heart blocks, the two-lead axis shortcut, which leads map to which coronary artery, and the classic electrolyte patterns. It is built for students, nurses, paramedics and monitor techs to scan or print. ECG and EKG are the same test. This sheet is a learning aid only. It is not for diagnosing patients, and real tracings always have to be read alongside the person's symptoms and history.

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS · Medical DoctorPMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-26
Quick answer

An EKG interpretation cheat sheet is a one-page reference to the key numbers and patterns. Normal adult values: heart rate 60–100 bpm, PR interval 120–200 ms, QRS under 120 ms, QTc under about 450 ms in men and 460–470 ms in women, and axis −30° to +90°. Read in this order: rate, rhythm, P waves, PR, QRS, axis, QT, ST-T.

EKG interpretation cheat sheet: the reading order

Use the same order on every tracing. Our step-by-step guide to how to read an EKG strip explains each step.

  1. Calibration (25 mm/s, 10 mm = 1 mV)
  2. Rate
  3. Rhythm (regular or irregular)
  4. P waves (present? one per QRS? upright in II?)
  5. PR interval
  6. QRS width
  7. Axis (12-lead)
  8. QT / QTc
  9. ST segments, T waves, Q waves, R-wave progression

Normal adult EKG values

Measurement Normal Abnormal flags
Heart rate 60–100 bpm < 60 bradycardia; > 100 tachycardia
P wave duration < 120 ms (3 small boxes) Wide or notched: left atrial enlargement
P wave height (II) < 2.5 mm ≥ 2.5 mm: right atrial enlargement
PR interval 120–200 ms (3–5 small boxes) > 200 ms: 1st-degree block; < 120 ms: pre-excitation or junctional
QRS duration < 120 ms (usually 80–100 ms) ≥ 120 ms: bundle branch block, ventricular or paced beat
QTc < ~450 ms men; < ~460–470 ms women > 500 ms: high risk of torsades
QRS axis −30° to +90° < −30° left axis deviation; > +90° right axis deviation
ST segment Level with baseline Elevation or depression: see ST rules below
T wave Upright in I, II, V3–V6; inverted in aVR Peaked, flat or inverted: see context
R-wave transition V3–V4 Poor R-wave progression

Upper QTc limits vary slightly between references. Full detail is in normal ECG values, and the QTc calculator does the correction for you.

Heart rate quick table

300 ÷ large boxes between R waves, or 1500 ÷ small boxes. For an irregular rhythm: QRS count in 6 s × 10. You can also use the ECG heart rate calculator.

Heart block recognition

Block Key feature Dropped beats?
1st-degree AV block PR > 200 ms, constant No
2nd-degree Mobitz I (Wenckebach) PR lengthens beat by beat until a QRS drops Yes, in a repeating pattern
2nd-degree Mobitz II PR constant; sudden dropped QRS Yes, unpredictably
2:1 AV block Every 2nd P wave has no QRS Yes; cannot tell type I from II on one strip
3rd-degree (complete) block P waves and QRS march independently; P-P and R-R each regular P waves never conduct
Right bundle branch block QRS ≥ 120 ms; rSR′ in V1; wide S in I and V6 —
Left bundle branch block QRS ≥ 120 ms; broad notched R in I, aVL, V5–V6; deep S in V1 —
Left anterior fascicular block Axis −45° or beyond; qR in aVL; rS in II, III, aVF —
Left posterior fascicular block Right axis; rS in I; qR in III (after excluding other causes) —

Axis quick rules (leads I and aVF)

Lead I Lead aVF Axis
Positive Positive Normal (0° to +90°)
Positive Negative Left axis. If lead II is positive, still normal (0° to −30°); if II is negative, left axis deviation
Negative Positive Right axis deviation
Negative Negative Extreme axis ("northwest"). Think ventricular rhythm or lead misplacement

STEMI territories

ST elevation of at least 1 mm in two contiguous leads. In V2–V3 the thresholds are ≥ 2 mm in men aged 40 and over, ≥ 2.5 mm in men under 40, and ≥ 1.5 mm in women.

Territory Leads Usual artery Reciprocal changes
Septal V1–V2 LAD —
Anterior V3–V4 LAD Inferior leads
Lateral I, aVL, V5–V6 Circumflex or diagonal III, aVF
Inferior II, III, aVF RCA (sometimes circumflex) I, aVL
Posterior ST depression + tall R in V1–V3; ST elevation in V7–V9 RCA or circumflex —
Right ventricle V4R (with inferior STEMI) Proximal RCA —

ST elevation in aVR with widespread ST depression suggests diffuse ischemia or left main disease. See ST elevation and STEMI vs NSTEMI.

Electrolytes and drugs

Condition EKG changes (in rough order of severity)
Hyperkalemia (high potassium) Peaked T → flat or lost P, long PR → wide QRS → sine wave → VF or asystole
Hypokalemia (low potassium) Flat T, ST depression, prominent U wave, long QU interval
Hypercalcemia (high calcium) Short QT (short ST segment)
Hypocalcemia (low calcium) Long QT (long ST segment)
Digoxin effect "Scooped" (sagging) ST depression, short QT

Mnemonics worth knowing

Mnemonic Use
300-150-100-75-60-50 Rate from large boxes between R waves
WiLLiaM MaRRoW LBBB: W in V1, M in V6. RBBB: M in V1, W in V6
"Longer, longer, longer, drop" Mobitz I (Wenckebach)
"Two thumbs up" Leads I and aVF both positive = normal axis
"Leaving / Returning" QRS in I and aVF pointing apart = left axis; pointing toward each other = right axis
"White on right, smoke over fire" US limb lead colours: white RA; black (smoke) LA above red (fire) LL
"Any wide-complex tachycardia is VT" Until proven otherwise

Red flags on any strip

  • Wide-complex tachycardia, VF, asystole or pulseless rhythms: start the emergency response
  • New ST elevation meeting the criteria above
  • Complete heart block or Mobitz II
  • QTc over 500 ms
  • Peaked T waves with a wide QRS (suspect severe hyperkalemia)

Get urgent help if a person has chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness or a racing heartbeat. Call 911 (US), 999 (UK) or 112 (EU). Do not rely on a cheat sheet in an emergency.

Frequently asked questions

What should an EKG cheat sheet include?

The normal intervals (PR, QRS, QTc), a rate table, rhythm and heart block features, the lead I and aVF axis rule, STEMI lead territories and the main electrolyte patterns.

What are the normal EKG intervals?

PR 120–200 ms, QRS under 120 ms, and QTc under about 450 ms in men or 460–470 ms in women. Above 500 ms is clearly prolonged.

What is the fastest way to calculate rate on an EKG?

For a regular rhythm, divide 300 by the number of large boxes between R waves. For an irregular rhythm, count QRS complexes in 6 seconds and multiply by 10.

Which leads show an inferior MI?

Leads II, III and aVF, usually from a blocked right coronary artery, often with reciprocal ST depression in I and aVL.

How do you tell Mobitz I from Mobitz II?

In Mobitz I the PR interval gets longer before a dropped beat. In Mobitz II the PR stays constant and a beat drops without warning. Mobitz II is more likely to progress to complete block.

Can I use an EKG cheat sheet to read my own EKG?

It can help you understand the terms, but it is not for self-diagnosis. Your clinician reads the tracing together with your symptoms, history and previous EKGs.
