Anterior Infarct, Age Undetermined: ECG Meaning

Needs clinician review

"Anterior infarct, age undetermined" means the software saw Q waves or missing R waves in the chest leads over the front of the heart (V2–V4), a pattern that a past heart attack can leave. It cannot say when, or be certain it happened at all. Lead placement, thick heart muscle and bundle branch block commonly mimic it.

What "anterior infarct age undetermined" means

The front wall of the left ventricle is supplied by the left anterior descending artery and is viewed by leads V2 to V4. Healthy muscle there produces an R wave that gets steadily taller from V1 to V5. If that growth fails, or a Q wave appears instead, the program suspects scar. When V1–V2 are also involved it prints anteroseptal infarct.

"Age undetermined" is a standard suffix. It tells you the machine did not see the ST-segment elevation of a heart attack in progress — only a fixed pattern that could be old. "Possible" and "cannot rule out" signal lower confidence.

What it looks like on the ECG

Leads V2–V4 show a Q wave or a QS complex, or R waves that stay very small across the chest leads instead of growing.

Common causes

  • Chest electrodes placed too high or too far apart
  • Left ventricular hypertrophy
  • Left bundle branch block or left anterior fascicular block
  • Emphysema and a vertical or rotated heart
  • A genuine previous anterior heart attack

Should you worry?

An anterior heart attack is a significant event, so this line should always be looked at by a clinician rather than ignored. But the ECG pattern alone is wrong often enough that you should not assume you have had one. Risk factors, symptoms and an echocardiogram decide the matter.

Get urgent help if this finding comes with chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a fast heartbeat that will not settle. Those symptoms matter more than any single ECG phrase.

What usually happens next

  1. Clinician over-read and comparison with previous tracings
  2. Repeat ECG with correct lead positions
  3. Echocardiogram to look for a thin or poorly moving front wall
  4. If scar is confirmed: risk-factor treatment and cardiology follow-up

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Is this pattern new compared with my old ECGs?
  • Should I have an echocardiogram?
  • If it is a false positive, how do I stop it alarming the next person who reads my ECG?

Frequently asked questions

Is an anterior infarct serious?

A real one is, because the front wall is a large part of the pumping chamber. The ECG statement on its own, however, is only a suspicion.

Can anterior infarct on ECG be wrong?

Yes. Poor R wave progression from lead placement or body build is a very common reason for this printout in healthy people.

What is the difference between anterior and septal infarct?

Only the leads involved. Septal refers to V1–V2, anterior to V3–V4, and anteroseptal to both.
